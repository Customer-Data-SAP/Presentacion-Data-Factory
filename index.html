The Challenge

Structural issues impacting pipeline growth

SAP LAC manages more than 166,000 corporate accounts across multiple countries and systems. Despite significant efforts in data cleansing and account creation, structural challenges continue to affect sales and marketing — causing teams to operate with incomplete, outdated, or inconsistent information that directly impacts segmentation, campaign effectiveness, and pipeline generation.
  • Inconsistent company classifications, revenue figures, and account ownership
  • Dependence on external providers with incomplete market coverage
  • Missing unique identifiers causing duplicate and inaccurate records
  • Manual and slow account creation processes
  • No unified criteria across regions — decisions vary by analyst
Mindset & Strategic Vision

Data is a constant asset.

The evolution of the Account Intelligence Factory goes beyond defining processes, tools, and production lines. It also implies a shift in mindset — positioning Data as an increasingly strategic, proactive, and business-connected function. Each production run is not just an operational task; it is an opportunity to generate intelligence that drives decisions, accelerates pipeline, and strengthens SAP's presence across LAC.

Strategic Vision

Move from a reactive Data Management model to an industrialized Account Intelligence Factory — a scalable data production line that transforms raw market information into sales-ready intelligence across Latin America.

Looking Ahead

Future Innovation Opportunities

Beyond the 6-stage production line, the Account Intelligence Factory can evolve into a more connected, traceable, and automated intelligence ecosystem. These are not additional production stages, but strategic capabilities that can increase scalability, trust, and business impact over time.

01
Account Knowledge Graph

Build a relationship-based intelligence layer connecting companies, subsidiaries, corporate groups, executives, industries, technologies, campaigns, opportunities, territories, and chambers of commerce.

Instead of treating each account as an isolated CRM record, the Knowledge Graph would allow SAP LAC to understand business ecosystems, ownership structures, market relationships, and hidden connections between accounts.

Expected value

Deeper market intelligence, better segmentation, stronger account prioritization, and improved visibility into corporate ecosystems.

02
Account Data Passport

Create a traceability layer for each account, showing where each data attribute came from, when it was last updated, which source validated it, and how reliable it is.

The Data Passport would document the origin and confidence level of key attributes such as Tax ID, website, legal status, revenue, employees, industry, address, executive information, and enrichment sources.

Expected value

Higher trust in CRM data, stronger governance, better auditability, and clearer decision-making for sales and marketing teams.

03
Agentic Data Operations

Introduce specialized AI agents to support operational orchestration across the data ecosystem. Agents could monitor sources, identify anomalies, validate changes, compare records, check governance rules, suggest next actions, and route exceptions for human review.

This would not replace the data strategy, but act as an automation layer that increases speed, consistency, and traceability.

Expected value

Higher automation, faster processing, lower manual workload, scalable data operations, and more consistent CRM updates.

The Ask

Establishing a Unified Way of Working

To scale the Account Intelligence Factory effectively, we need alignment on how regions operate, collaborate, and make decisions — establishing a single, consistent standard across LAC.

01
Trust in Official & Governmental Data Sources
Government APIs and official registries are our primary sources of truth — legally grounded and auditable. We ask global teams to apply less restrictive validation thresholds, trusting these official datasets as sufficient evidence of data accuracy.
02
Defined SLAs & Delivery Timelines
Clear deadlines and service-level agreements for each production stage — so every region knows when deliverables are due, reducing delays and enabling reliable planning across the factory.
03
Standardized Processes
Establish a single way of working across LAC, with shared account classification criteria, data quality thresholds, coverage targets, enrichment priorities, and standardized production processes. All regions should follow the same workflow — from discovery and validation through enrichment and CRM governance — while operating with shared tools, comparable outputs, and consistent cross-regional coordination.
04
Common Criteria, Standardized Processes & Cross-Regional Operations
Shared standards for account classification and data quality — applied through a unified production workflow that all regions follow, enabling better coordination, shared tooling, and comparable outputs across LAC.
Our Response

Proposals

Concrete initiatives the LAC Account Intelligence Factory is putting forward to address each of the identified gaps.

01
Establish Government APIs as the Validation Standard
Formalize official government registries and APIs as the primary validation layer for all LAC accounts — and work with global teams to recognize this evidence tier, reducing unnecessary re-validation overhead for the regional factory.
02
Introduce Stage-Level SLA Tracking
Define delivery deadlines per production stage and implement lightweight tracking so teams can monitor on-time completion, flag delays early, and report pipeline health to stakeholders with confidence.
03
Implement a Unified LAC Operating Framework
Co-create and implement a single operating framework for all LAC country teams, combining shared account classification rules, data quality standards, enrichment priorities, and the standardized Discovery, Update, and Inactivation pipelines. Support this model with documented processes, shared tooling, recurring cross-regional reviews, common dashboards, and a shared backlog — ensuring that every country operates under the same standards while remaining aligned on priorities, blockers, and production status.
04
Build a Unified LAC Operating Model
Co-create a shared classification rulebook, standardize the 3-pipeline production model across all LAC regions, and establish a bi-monthly cross-regional sync — ensuring every team operates with the same criteria, tooling, and rhythm.
